Early evidence

Google DeepMind’s GNoME predicted 2.2 million new crystal structures (2023). Microsoft and PNNL used AI to screen 32 million candidates and make a new battery electrolyte (2024). AI-designed drug candidates are progressing through clinical trials. AI-generated layouts are used in Google’s TPU chips. Network digital twins are entering planning and connections work.

What AI does not compress

Factories, clinical trials, safety cases, standards, spectrum, planning consent and grid connections. That is why physical fields shrink less than software, and why regulation and assurance increasingly set the pace.

About this model

Each arc traces one breakthrough from the first published or demonstrated research to its first commercial product, operational use or binding change. Lanes group arcs by where the research began (universities, industry labs, or government and publicly funded labs) or by field. The height of each arc is the gap in years.

Definitions

  • Research is the first credible publication, patent or demonstration of the core idea.
  • Change is the first commercial sale, operational deployment, or rule taking effect. It is not mass adoption, which usually takes longer.
  • Trend lines join the median gap for each lane in each era: before 1950, 1950–69, 1970–89, 1990–2009 and 2010 onwards.
  • Outlook arcs are indicative judgements for 2026–2031, each with a confidence level. They are not forecasts.

Caveats

  • Dates are approximate and some attributions are contested; many breakthroughs drew on all three sources.
  • The sample is curated, not exhaustive, and only includes ideas that succeeded.
  • Physical and safety-critical sectors stay slower than software because of capital, safety and regulatory cycles. That is often appropriate.
