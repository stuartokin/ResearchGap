About this model

Each arc traces one breakthrough from the first published or demonstrated research to its first commercial or operational use. Arcs sit in three lanes by where the originating research was done: universities, industry labs, or government and publicly funded labs. The height of each arc is the gap in years.

Definitions

  • Research is the first credible publication, patent or lab demonstration of the core idea.
  • Product is the first commercial sale or operational deployment, not mass adoption. Adoption usually takes longer.
  • Trend lines join the median gap for each lane in each era: before 1950, 1950–69, 1970–89, 1990–2009 and 2010 onwards.
  • Outlook arcs are indicative judgements for 2026–2031, each marked with a confidence level. They are not forecasts.

Caveats

  • Dates are approximate and some attributions are contested; many breakthroughs drew on all three sources.
  • The sample is curated rather than exhaustive, and it only includes ideas that succeeded.
  • Physical sectors (energy, medicines) remain slower than software because of capital, safety and regulatory cycles. This is often appropriate.

Main references

  • Nobel Prize citations (physics, chemistry, medicine) for origin dates.
  • NCSC, Timelines for migration to post-quantum cryptography (2025).
  • NIST FIPS 203, 204 and 205 (August 2024).
  • Company and institutional histories: Bell Labs, IBM Research, Xerox PARC, CERN, DARPA, MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology.
  • Published vendor roadmaps, treated as aspirations rather than facts.
